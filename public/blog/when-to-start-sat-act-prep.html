When Should Your Child Start SAT/ACT Prep?

Timing guidance by grade, and what to prioritize before diving into full practice tests.

The most common mistake in test prep isn't starting too late — it's starting with the wrong thing at the right time. Here's a grade-by-grade way to think about it.

Grades 8-9: build the foundation, skip the test-specific drilling

At this stage, the highest-leverage work is strengthening core algebra, reading comprehension, and grammar — the material the SAT/ACT actually test, just not in test format yet. Formal test prep this early tends to produce burnout without much score benefit, since the content foundation isn't fully in place.

Grade 10: a good time for a low-stakes diagnostic

A single practice test (many students take the PSAT through school) gives a real baseline without pressure. Use it to identify whether reading, math, or pacing is the bigger gap — that answer should shape everything that comes next, rather than generic prep.

Grade 11: this is prime prep time

Most students benefit most from focused prep in 11th grade — enough content foundation is in place, and there's still runway before applications. This is when structured, regular sessions (not just practice tests) tend to move scores the most, because there's time to actually fix identified weak spots rather than just re-test the same gaps.

Grade 12 fall: refine, don't rebuild

By senior fall, prep should shift to timing, endurance, and polishing weak question types — not relearning fundamentals. If foundational gaps are still showing up this late, it's worth treating that as a signal to prioritize subject tutoring alongside (or instead of) pure test-format practice.

The one thing that matters more than timing

Consistent, regular sessions beat a single intense cram, at any grade. A student doing 45 focused minutes twice a week for two months will typically outperform one doing an 8-hour weekend cram the week before the test.
